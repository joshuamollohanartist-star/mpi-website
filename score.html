Understanding Your Score

The MPI Artist Score is a 0–100 performance index built specifically for independent artists. It analyzes your Spotify data through the Chartmetric API and translates six weighted metrics into a single, clear number. The goal isn't to rate you — it's to show you exactly where you stand and where your biggest growth opportunities are hiding. Think of it as your career health check.
Your score is a weighted composite of six Spotify data points pulled via the Chartmetric API:

Streams per Listener — 30% (the single biggest factor — how engaged your audience actually is)
Audience Quality — 20% (follower-to-listener ratio and engagement signals)
Listener Growth — 20% (month-over-month trajectory)
Playlist Footprint — 15% (reach and editorial placement)
Follower Growth — 10% (momentum in your fanbase)
Market Position — 5% (Spotify popularity relative to your tier)

Every component is normalized so that 100 isn't a ceiling reserved for superstars — it reflects excellence at your level of career.
Elite (80–100) — Exceptional engagement across every metric. Your audience is highly active and your growth momentum is strong. You're operating at a professional level.

Strong (65–79) — Above-average performance with clear, identifiable strengths. A few targeted improvements could push you into Elite territory.

Developing (50–64) — Solid foundation with real gaps to close. You have an audience — now it's about deepening engagement and widening your reach.

Early Stage (35–49) — Building momentum. The fundamentals are in place; consistency and strategic playlisting will move the needle most at this stage.

Getting Started (0–34) — You're in the early chapters. Focus on release cadence, playlist pitching, and converting casual listeners into repeat fans before scaling.
The MPI Score is built to reward engagement quality, not vanity metrics. An artist with 50,000 monthly listeners and a 4.2 streams-per-listener ratio will often outscore an artist with 500,000 listeners who mostly skip after one play. If your score feels low, look at streams per listener and audience quality first — those two components make up 50% of your total score and are the most common gaps we see.
High streams don't automatically mean high engagement. If you have a large passive listener base — people who hear your music once on a playlist but never return — your streams-per-listener ratio will be low, and that's our highest-weighted metric at 30%. The score is designed to flag exactly this pattern. The fix: focus on converting passive listeners into active fans through consistent releases, deeper catalog, and direct-to-fan promotion.

Data & Privacy

We pull publicly available Spotify artist data — monthly listeners, follower count, Spotify popularity score, playlist placements, and release history — via the Spotify Web API and Chartmetric analytics. We also calculate derived metrics like streams-per-listener ratio and month-over-month growth rate from that data. We never access your personal streaming history, payment information, or private account data.
The score tool itself does not permanently store your raw Spotify data. If you choose to unlock your full report by entering your email, we save your score, artist name, and Spotify URL so we can send you your report and follow up with relevant MPI resources. We don't sell your data. Ever. We're artists too — we built this for us, not for ad networks.

Improving Your Score

Attack the highest-weighted components first. In order of impact:

1. Streams per Listener (30%) — Release more music so loyal fans have a deeper catalog to stream. A two-track EP gives fans less to return to than a 10-song album.
2. Audience Quality (20%) — Convert passive playlist listeners into followers with strong artist bio copy, social links, and call-to-actions on your music.
3. Listener Growth (20%) — Consistent release cadence is the most reliable driver. Pitching editorial playlists and running targeted ads around release week accelerates this fastest.

The fix is two-pronged: build a deeper catalog so there's more to listen to, and improve first-listen quality so people hit repeat. Also check whether your listeners are mostly coming from passive playlist adds — those tend to inflate raw listener numbers without building real fans.

But the relationship goes deeper than that — playlist placements drive listener growth and can indirectly improve your streams-per-listener ratio if the right audience finds you. Quality matters more than quantity: landing on one well-curated, genre-relevant playlist with 50,000 followers will do more for your score than being on 200 low-engagement ones. Pitch Spotify editorial playlists through Spotify for Artists and cultivate relationships with independent curators in your genre.
